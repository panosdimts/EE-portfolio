Metal Detector PCB

PCB design · analog circuits

An oscillator-based metal detector circuit turned into a fabricated PCB, built from ALD1103 MOSFETs with two LC oscillators, a mixer, and an RC low-pass filter. I assembled the board and debugged it stage by stage; the fixed oscillator measured 50.2 kHz against 50.27 kHz in LTspice.

Summary

Schematic to two-layer layout to a fabricated board, hand-soldered and debugged on a scope stage by stage. The finished detector drives an 8 Ω speaker with an audible beat note.

Tools

Altium Designer LTspice PCB layout ALD1103 / MOSFET analog Hand soldering Oscilloscope debugging

Overview

Two LC oscillators built from ALD1103 MOSFETs, one fixed and one tuned by the search coil, are mixed down to an audio-rate beat note and low-pass filtered to drive a speaker. A nearby metal object pulls the search coil's inductance, the beat frequency shifts, and the tone changes. This project took that circuit all the way to a fabricated board.

Design process

  • Took the schematic to a two-layer board in Altium, with test points left on each stage so the oscillators, the mixer and the filter could be probed separately.
  • Kept the two oscillators apart on the board, so the fixed one could not pull the search-coil one into lock and kill the beat note.
  • Hand-soldered and inspected the fabricated board, then brought it up stage by stage instead of powering the whole thing at once.
  • Probed each stage against LTspice: the fixed oscillator measured 50.2 kHz against 50.27 kHz simulated, 0.14% off.
