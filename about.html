About

I’m Panagiotis Dimtsoudis, Panos to most people, an electrical engineering student at the University of Pennsylvania. I’m in Penn’s submatriculation program, earning the B.S.E. and the M.S.E. together.

My focus is transistor-level IC design and custom layout, plus the hardware around it: analog boards, embedded firmware, and device measurement.

I grew up in northeastern Greece, repairing equipment with my grandfather. That is where the hardware started.

What I work on

  • Digital IC design and VLSI

    Transistor-level design in Cadence Virtuoso: sizing from hand analysis and logical effort, custom layout that passes DRC and LVS, Quantus extraction, and post-layout simulation in Spectre. Most recently a 6T SRAM bitcell, a 16 × 4 macro, and the pipelined MAC datapath built around them.

    SRAM MAC accelerator →

  • Analog and PCB hardware

    Schematic capture and board layout in Altium, simulation in LTspice, then assembly and bench work. I route the board, solder it, and debug it stage by stage on a scope: oscillators, instrumentation front ends, filters, and audio circuits.

    Metal detector PCB →

  • Device characterization

    Research in Penn’s Device Research and Engineering Laboratory, measuring ferroelectric junctions from 14 to 400 K on cryogenic and high-temperature probe stations: J–V, C–V, PUND, breakdown and endurance. I write the measurement sequences, the Python that extracts device parameters from the exports, and the 3D models of the device stack.

    AlScN ferroelectric junctions →
