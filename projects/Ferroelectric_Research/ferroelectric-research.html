AlScN Ferroelectric Junctions

Device research · cryogenic memory · compute-in-memory

Ultrathin all-nitride ferroelectric junctions, 1 nm AlN / 4 nm AlScN / 1 nm AlN between aluminum electrodes, measured from 400 down to 14 K in Penn’s Device Research and Engineering Laboratory. I run the measurements and write the analysis; the group’s results are below. The paper itself is a manuscript in preparation and is not public yet.

Summary

Can a ferroelectric only 6 nm thick keep switching, and keep a readable resistance state, from elevated temperature all the way down to cryogenic operation? The group’s answer for this all-nitride stack is yes, from 400 to 14 K.

Tools

Cryogenic probe station High-temperature probe station Python 3D CAD

Overview

Aluminum scandium nitride (AlScN) is a wurtzite nitride ferroelectric with strong polarization, thermal stability, and sputter-based back-end-of-line compatibility. This work studies monolithic all-nitride Al / AlN / AlScN / AlN / Al junctions built around a 1 nm AlN / 4 nm AlScN / 1 nm AlN active stack, and asks whether a 6 nm heterostructure can preserve ferroelectric switching and a nonvolatile resistance readout from elevated temperature down to cryogenic operation.

What I did

  • Operated the cryogenic and high-temperature probe stations for the device measurements.
  • Ran the DC and AC J–V, C–V, PUND, breakdown and endurance measurements used in the analysis, and wrote the measurement sequences so every device and temperature point is captured the same way.
  • Collected and organized the temperature-dependent characterization data.
  • Analyzed switching, hysteresis, breakdown, capacitance, polarization, endurance, ON/OFF ratio and read-current trends across temperature.
  • Wrote the Python for data processing, visualization, parameter extraction and device-to-device comparison.
  • Built the device visuals: the cross-section in Fig. 2, and 3D CAD models of the device stack and of an 8 × 8 crossbar array for the compute-in-memory work.

Every measurement is repeated across the 14 to 400 K range, on a cryogenic station at the low end and a high-temperature station at the top, so the same device can be compared with itself across temperature.

What we found

Coercive field≈ 5.4 MV/cmAt 14 K
Breakdown field≈ 11.5 MV/cmUnder 10 kHz operation

Temperature- and field-dependent transport point to a tunnel-dominant, local trap-assisted readout mechanism, rather than direct tunneling through the full stack or classical Poole–Frenkel emission alone.

Compute-in-memory

Beyond binary memory, the measured conductance states serve as programmable analog weights for vector-matrix multiplication. Multilevel conductance programming made possible a repetition-code syndrome decoder, built from measured devices, for detecting and decoding qubit errors. Together with the cryogenic operation, that points to scaled all-nitride ferroelectric junctions as candidates for memory and local compute next to quantum-control hardware.

Device models

I also built 3D CAD models of the device stack and of an 8 × 8 crossbar array of these junctions, for the compute-in-memory work.
